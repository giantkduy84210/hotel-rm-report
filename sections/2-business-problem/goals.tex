\subsection{Expected Goals}

The expected goals of the decision support system are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Primary goal --- maximise RevPAR}: the system should recommend pricing, allocation, and overbooking decisions that maximise Revenue Per Available Room, the composite industry-standard metric that balances ADR and occupancy~\cite{goeldner2009}.
  \item \textbf{Secondary goal --- reduce vacant rooms in the low season}: by adjusting prices dynamically and targeting price-sensitive segments, the system should help improve occupancy during periods of low demand without permanently eroding rate positioning.
  \item \textbf{Constraint --- limit walked guests}: the expected number of guests denied a room due to overbooking must not exceed a management-specified threshold per night, protecting guest experience and brand reputation. This constraint reflects the textbook's warning that ``\emph{should the quality of facilities and services drop, demand will fall off---a serious blow to the tourism industry in the area}'' (p.~388).
\end{enumerate}

All three goals are \emph{measurable} from the data warehouse: RevPAR and occupancy are computed from the fact tables, and the walked-guest constraint is an output of the overbooking model. This aligns with the project requirement that the business problem be defined with a context, decision makers, and expected goals that are verifiable from available data.
